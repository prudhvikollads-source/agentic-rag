# E-Commerce Inventory Management

Inventory systems track how many units of each product are available to promise to customers. The available-to-promise figure is the stock physically on hand, minus units already reserved by open orders, plus confirmed inbound stock that will arrive before the delivery date.

Safety stock is the buffer quantity held above expected demand. Safety stock protects the store against uncertainty in both customer demand and supplier lead time: if a shipment is delayed or a promotion sells faster than forecast, the buffer absorbs the difference and the product stays in stock. The larger the variability in demand or lead time, the more safety stock a planner holds.

A reorder point combines lead time and safety stock. When the stock level falls to the reorder point — the demand expected during the supplier lead time, plus the safety stock — a purchase order is raised so replenishment arrives before the shelves empty.

Multi-warehouse stores allocate orders to the warehouse closest to the customer that holds the stock. Splitting one order across two warehouses is avoided where possible because it doubles the delivery cost and often produces two parcels arriving on different days.

Stock counts are verified with cycle counting, where a small subset of products is counted every day on a rotating schedule. Cycle counting finds record errors continuously and avoids the disruption of a full annual stocktake that closes the warehouse.
